# The Four Calls -- Scripts

Student-facing (once recorded/presented) but this file itself is
authoring material -- see `README.md` for the overall experience shape,
backend spec, and production checklist. Built by Prompt 018
(`../../prompts/018_the_four_calls_world_selection_experience.md`),
drawing character voice directly from the Prompt 019 dossiers in
`../four_faces/`.

Each call answers exactly three questions and no more: what kind of
place is this, what kinds of problems find you here, would you enjoy
coming back all semester. No future plot, no full cast, no delayed
payoffs are revealed. Runtime target: 45-90s each.

---

## Call One: Frontier Settlement

- **Speaker/character:** Garrett Boone (see `../four_faces/frontier_garrett_boone.md`).
- **Intended setting:** A plain desk, a ledger open on it, minimal
  background -- suggest "office," not "set."
- **Performance direction:** Unhurried. Let pauses land. He's mid-task
  when we find him; he doesn't perform for the camera, he just happens
  to look up.
- **Props/wardrobe:** Suspenders, rolled sleeves, reading glasses
  pushed up, a ledger book.
- **Approximate runtime:** 60s.

**Spoken script:**

> *(Writing in the ledger. Doesn't look up right away.)*
>
> "Four hundred people. That's Docket Creek now. Started with four
> families and a well.
>
> *(sets down the pen, looks up)*
>
> Everything that used to live in someone's head -- who's owed what,
> whose claim is whose, how much water's left for who -- somebody has
> to write that down now, and write it down *right*. That's the job.
> Not glamorous. Matters more than almost anything else in this town.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> Funny thing happened at the mill last week -- grain's coming up short
> in the count, nobody can say why yet. Not the first odd number I've
> seen. Won't be the last.
>
> If you're the kind of person who likes building something that has
> to actually hold -- not just look good, *hold* -- this is where you
> belong. Come find me at the Recorder's Office."

**On-screen text during call:** "DOCKET CREEK -- FRONTIER SETTLEMENT"
(opening); "THE RECORDER'S OFFICE" (closing, over title card).

**Alternate line (for a shorter cut, ~35s):** Cut everything between
"That's the job" and "If you're the kind of person" -- keeps the
premise and the hook, drops the mill-grain aside.

**What the student should understand after viewing:** Docket Creek is
a growing settlement whose informal, memory-based systems are breaking
down; the work is building durable, trustworthy record-keeping under
real practical constraints; the tone is grounded and plainspoken, not
theatrical.

**Tiny hook planted:** The mill's grain count coming up short. Subtle,
unexplained, does not need to resolve. See `README.md`'s "Hooks
introduced" for the continuity-ledger cross-reference.

## Call Two: Investigation Bureau

- **Speaker/character:** Chief Nora Arana (see `../four_faces/investigation_chief_arana.md`).
- **Intended setting:** A desk, a closed case folder in front of her,
  minimal background -- suggest "records office," not "interrogation
  room."
- **Performance direction:** Measured and economical. She doesn't
  perform urgency; the stakes come through in precision, not volume.
- **Props/wardrobe:** A badge, a plain blazer, glasses, a case folder.
- **Approximate runtime:** 55s.

**Spoken script:**

> *(Seated, folder closed in front of her.)*
>
> "This division doesn't run on hunches. It runs on records that agree
> with each other.
>
> *(opens the folder, doesn't look inside yet)*
>
> Most days, that's straightforward. Some days, two reports come back
> about the same event, and they don't match. That's not a mystery --
> that's a bug. And finding it before it becomes someone's bad day in
> court is the actual job.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> One of our field investigators filed a report this week with a
> timestamp that doesn't quite line up with what's on file. Probably
> nothing. I'd like to be sure.
>
> If you're the kind of person who wants to know *why* something's
> true, not just *that* it's true -- Case Records Division could use
> you."

**On-screen text during call:** "MERIDIAN CASE BUREAU -- INVESTIGATION"
(opening); "CASE RECORDS DIVISION" (closing, over title card).

**Alternate line (for a shorter cut, ~35s):** Cut the "one of our field
investigators" aside entirely -- keeps the premise and the ask, drops
the hook (use only when a shorter cut is required and the hook can
wait for a different call).

**What the student should understand after viewing:** the Bureau's
work is about verifying and reconciling records, not solving dramatic
mysteries; the tone is quiet, professional, procedural; the stakes are
real but calm.

**Tiny hook planted:** The field report's mismatched timestamp.
Subtle, unexplained, does not need to resolve.

## Call Three: Starship Log

- **Speaker/character:** Commander Elias Rourke (see `../four_faces/starship_commander_rourke.md`).
- **Intended setting:** Standing, simple background suggesting "bridge"
  without needing an actual set -- a plain wall, good lighting, is
  enough.
- **Performance direction:** Calm, forward-leaning, genuinely curious.
  He's briefing a new crew member, not delivering a speech.
- **Props/wardrobe:** Simple utilitarian jacket or ship-branded
  pullover, name badge/patch with an original (non-franchise) ship
  crest, a tablet.
- **Approximate runtime:** 55s.

**Spoken script:**

> *(Standing, tablet in hand, glances at it, then up.)*
>
> "RSV Kestrel. Six weeks out from the nearest relay, mapping territory
> nobody's fully charted yet.
>
> *(sets tablet down)*
>
> Out here, when something goes wrong, there's no calling home to ask
> what to do. The ship's systems have to hold on their own -- the log,
> the sensors, all of it has to actually agree with itself, because
> we're too far out to double-check by hand.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> Sensor's been reading something odd the last two days. Probably
> nothing. Probably.
>
> If you want to help keep a real mission running -- not just build
> something impressive, build something that *holds* when nobody's
> watching -- we've got a berth open. Systems Officer, RSV Kestrel."

**On-screen text during call:** "RSV KESTREL -- STARSHIP LOG" (opening);
"SYSTEMS OFFICER, RSV KESTREL" (closing, over title card).

**Alternate line (for a shorter cut, ~35s):** Cut "Sensor's been
reading something odd the last two days. Probably nothing. Probably."
-- keeps the premise and the ask, drops the hook.

**What the student should understand after viewing:** the Kestrel is
a working research/survey ship, not a combat vessel; the work is
building operational systems the crew can trust at real distance; the
tone is calm, competent, forward-looking.

**Tiny hook planted:** The two-day-old odd sensor reading. Subtle,
unexplained, does not need to resolve.

## Call Four: Small Business

- **Speaker/character:** Frank Delgado (see `../four_faces/small_business_frank_delgado.md`).
- **Intended setting:** A back-office counter, order pad in hand,
  minimal background suggesting "hardware store back room."
- **Performance direction:** Warm, practical, a little tired-but-fond.
  He's talking the way he'd talk to a promising new hire.
- **Props/wardrobe:** Shop apron or vest, reading glasses, order pad.
- **Approximate runtime:** 55s.

**Spoken script:**

> *(At the counter, order pad in hand.)*
>
> "Twenty-two years I've run this place. Delgado Hardware and Supply.
>
> *(sets the pad down)*
>
> Ran it on this pad and what's in my head, mostly. Worked fine when
> it was just me and Yolanda up front. Doesn't work anymore -- had a
> supplier ask me last week if I even knew what I had in stock. Had to
> tell him I wasn't sure. That's not a good answer to give a supplier.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> Inventory's come up short twice this month too. Might be nothing.
> Might not be.
>
> If you're the kind of person who likes making something real
> actually work better -- not a made-up business, a real one, with
> real people depending on it -- come find me in the back office."

**On-screen text during call:** "DELGADO HARDWARE \& SUPPLY -- SMALL
BUSINESS" (opening); "THE BACK OFFICE" (closing, over title card).

**Alternate line (for a shorter cut, ~35s):** Cut "Inventory's come up
short twice this month too. Might be nothing. Might not be." -- keeps
the premise and the ask, drops the hook.

**What the student should understand after viewing:** Delgado Hardware
is a real, functioning small business whose informal systems are
buckling under real growth; the work is building tools that actually
help at the counter and in the back office; the tone is grounded,
warm, practical.

**Tiny hook planted:** Inventory coming up short twice this month.
Subtle, unexplained, does not need to resolve.

## Accessibility

Every script above is written for spoken delivery and doubles as its
own caption-ready transcript verbatim -- no additional condensation
needed for captions; use the spoken script text as-is for burned-in or
soft captions. On-screen text (title cards) is specified separately
per call so it can be read independently of the audio track. No sound
cue beyond spoken voice is used in any of the four calls, so no
accessible-equivalent guidance is needed for this version.
