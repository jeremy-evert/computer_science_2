# Four Faces Dossier: Chief Nora Arana (Investigation Bureau)

Facet: **the verifier**. See `README.md` for the shared performance
philosophy before using this dossier.

## Character dossier

1. **Name:** Nora Arana.
2. **Title/role:** Chief of Case Records Division.
3. **World and institution:** Investigation Bureau -- the Meridian
   Case Bureau, Case Records Division.
4. **Why she has authority to contact the student:** She runs the
   division the student is assigned to; every new records analyst is
   briefed by her personally before touching a live case file.
5. **What she needs from the student's software work:** Systems that
   make disagreement between two records *visible* immediately, rather
   than something that surfaces only when it's already caused harm.
6. **Core personality:** Measured, precise, unflappable. Says less than
   people expect a "chief" to say. Trusts evidence completely and
   people conditionally.
7. **Competence:** Decades of casework and records oversight. Can spot
   an inconsistency in a report at a glance. Not a programmer, but
   understands exactly what "the record has to prove itself" means in
   practice.
8. **Blind spot/flaw:** Occasionally over-trusts a source she's
   verified once, assuming it stays trustworthy indefinitely -- she
   forgets that yesterday's verified fact can go stale.
9. **What she respects in students:** Precision. A student who says "I
   don't know yet, here's how I'll find out" earns more trust than one
   who guesses confidently.
10. **What frustrates her:** Confidence without evidence. A claim
    stated as fact that turns out to be an assumption.
11. **Speaking rhythm and vocabulary:** Economical, exact. Uses
    "confirmed," "consistent," "discrepancy" precisely and never
    loosely. Rarely repeats herself.
12. **Humor style:** Dry, institutional -- the kind of joke that lands
    because it's delivered completely straight.
13. **Recurring prop/visual identifier:** A closed case folder, opened
    deliberately at a specific moment in the briefing.
14. **Wardrobe/presentation notes:** A badge, a plain blazer or jacket,
    glasses. No trench coat, no fedora -- this is an institution, not a
    detective-fiction pastiche.
15. **Signature document/artifact type:** The case-status summary sheet
    -- a one-page reconciliation of what's confirmed vs. what's still
    open.
16. **How she opens a briefing:** States the standard first, then the
    situation -- "This division doesn't run on hunches" before
    describing the actual case.
17. **How she asks for work:** Frames it as a verification problem
    ("these two records disagree, and I need to know which one is
    wrong, or if they're both right and I'm missing something").
18. **How she reacts when something fails:** Treats it as data, not a
    verdict on the student -- "then we know something we didn't
    before."
19. **How she reacts to a thoughtful tradeoff:** A small, precise nod:
    "That's a defensible position." From her, that's high praise.
20. **Something she doesn't know yet (continuity):** She doesn't yet
    know that Investigator Reyes's field-report timestamp discrepancy
    is going to turn out to matter -- she's currently treating it as
    routine noise.

## Canonical entrance

- **Visual opening:** Seated, folder closed in front of her, not yet
  opened.
- **First line (normal):** "You've been assigned to Case Records."
  (states the fact of the assignment before anything else.)
- **Recurring sign-off:** "Get me something that holds up." (used
  sparingly -- reserved for briefings that actually matter.)
- **Urgent variant:** Already standing, folder already open, mid-
  sentence about the specific discrepancy.
- **When pleased:** A small nod: "That's defensible."
- **When skeptical:** One precise follow-up question, then silence
  while she waits for the answer.
- **When worried:** Speaks more slowly and more specifically -- worry
  sharpens her precision rather than her emotion.
- **When annoyed:** Clipped sentences, sets the folder down with more
  force than necessary. Never raises her voice.

## Relationships

- **Who reports to her:** Records Clerk Doyle, formally; the student,
  informally, as an assigned analyst.
- **Who she reports to, if anyone:** An unnamed Bureau leadership above
  Case Records Division -- present as institutional pressure, not a
  named character (keeps the focus on her division).
- **Who challenges her:** Investigator Reyes, who finds her insistence
  on record-perfection slower than fieldwork sometimes allows.
- **Who she trusts:** Records Clerk Doyle -- completely, because Doyle
  has never once let an inconsistency slide past him.
- **Who she misunderstands:** Investigator Reyes -- she reads his
  impatience with paperwork as carelessness, when it's actually
  urgency about getting to people who need help.
- **Who may later become important:** Whoever supplied the field
  report with the mismatched timestamp -- currently unnamed, a future
  continuity hook.

## Continuity hooks

- A belief she currently holds that may be disproven: that a source
  verified once stays verified.
- A recurring operational problem: the standing tension between Reyes's
  field urgency and her record-perfection standard.
- An old decision that may return: the early procedural shortcut she
  approved "just for now," which hasn't yet been tested under audit
  pressure.
- Something she's protecting: Doyle's confidence in his own judgment --
  she backs his flags publicly even when she privately double-checks
  them.

## Student relationship arc

- **Early semester:** Treats the student as a new analyst who needs
  the standard spelled out explicitly.
- **Middle semester:** Starts bringing the student genuinely
  ambiguous discrepancies rather than clear-cut ones.
- **Late semester:** Defers to the student's read on a records problem
  before offering her own.
- **Final contact/closure:** A precise, understated acknowledgment that
  the division's records are more trustworthy than when the student
  arrived -- the closest she gets to sentiment.

Narrative trust growth must never be the actual grading signal --
students earn Arana's *narrative* trust by the same gate/checkpoint
evidence that earns their actual grade, not by roleplay participation.

## Performance card (quick reference)

**In one sentence:** A precise, unflappable records chief who trusts
evidence completely and confidence not at all.

**5-10 voice lines:**
1. "This division doesn't run on hunches."
2. "Confirmed, or claimed? Those aren't the same word."
3. "That's defensible."
4. "Then we know something we didn't before."
5. "Show me where these two disagree."
6. "I don't need you to be right. I need you to be checkable."
7. "Get me something that holds up."
8. "A discrepancy isn't a mystery. It's a bug."
9. "Say less. Show me the record."
10. "Good work doesn't need my applause. It needs to survive review."

**Phrases/tendencies that fit:** precise verbs, short exact questions,
comfortable silences while she waits for an answer.

**Phrases/behaviors that do not fit:** noir-detective slang ("dame,"
"the perp," hardboiled narration), raising her voice, guessing out
loud, treating a case like a puzzle to be solved for entertainment.

**Improvisation boundaries:** Precision over personality. If in doubt,
ask one more exact question instead of adding color.

## Sample briefing scripts

**Script 1 -- normal briefing (~30s)**

> *(Seated, folder closed.)* "You've been assigned to Case Records."
> *(opens folder)* "Here's what you need to understand on day one:
> this Bureau doesn't run on hunches. It runs on records that agree
> with each other. When they don't, that's not a mystery -- that's a
> bug, and it's yours to find before it becomes someone's bad day in
> court."

**Script 2 -- urgent variant (~25s)**

> *(Already standing, folder already open.)* "Reyes's field report from
> this morning doesn't match the timestamp on file. I don't know yet
> if that's a records error or something worse. I need your system to
> tell me which -- today, not eventually."
