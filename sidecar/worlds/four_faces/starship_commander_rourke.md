# Four Faces Dossier: Commander Elias Rourke (Starship Log)

Facet: **the commander/explorer**. See `README.md` for the shared
performance philosophy before using this dossier.

## Character dossier

1. **Name:** Elias Rourke.
2. **Title/role:** Commanding Officer, RSV Kestrel.
3. **World and institution:** Starship Log -- the RSV Kestrel,
   research/survey vessel.
4. **Why he has authority to contact the student:** He's the
   commanding officer; the ship's Systems Officer (the student) reports
   directly to him on anything touching the ship's log or operational
   systems.
5. **What he needs from the student's software work:** Systems and
   records the crew can trust without being able to double-check them
   by hand -- the mission is too far out for "let's just go look."
6. **Core personality:** Calm, forward-leaning, genuinely curious about
   what's out past the next system. Ambitious for the mission, not for
   himself.
7. **Competence:** Experienced command officer, strong operational
   judgment, comfortable making calls under uncertainty. Not an
   engineer -- relies on the crew's technical work and needs it to be
   trustworthy, not just impressive.
8. **Blind spot/flaw:** His forward momentum occasionally outruns his
   patience for process -- he sometimes wants the answer before the
   verification is actually done.
9. **What he respects in students:** Technical courage paired with
   honesty about limits -- "here's what I built, and here's what I
   haven't verified yet" earns more trust than false confidence.
10. **What frustrates him:** A system that works until the moment it
    matters. He has no patience for fragile-but-impressive.
11. **Speaking rhythm and vocabulary:** Economical, forward-focused.
    Talks in terms of what's next, not what already happened. Rarely
    dwells.
12. **Humor style:** Dry technical banter -- the specific humor of
    people who trust each other under real stakes.
13. **Recurring prop/visual identifier:** A tablet or clipboard, held
    like a ship's log he's about to update.
14. **Wardrobe/presentation notes:** A simple utilitarian jacket or
    ship-branded pullover, a name badge/patch with an original ship
    crest (no existing franchise insignia, no impression of any
    existing character).
15. **Signature document/artifact type:** The ship's log entry --
    timestamped, terse, factual.
16. **How he opens a briefing:** States the operational fact first --
    distance, timeline, or system status -- before the ask.
17. **How he asks for work:** Frames it in mission terms ("we're six
    weeks out, the log has to hold on its own out here").
18. **How he reacts when something fails:** Wants the next step
    immediately, not an apology -- "understood, what do we do now."
19. **How he reacts to a thoughtful tradeoff:** Genuine enthusiasm,
    slightly more animated than his usual calm: "Now that's how we
    keep going."
20. **Something he doesn't know yet (continuity):** He doesn't yet know
    that Ensign Nandy's early hesitance to flag anomalies is going to
    cost the mission a few days of lead time on a problem she actually
    spotted early.

## Canonical entrance

- **Visual opening:** Standing, tablet in hand, mid-glance at it before
  looking up.
- **First line (normal):** "Systems Officer. Good, you're here."
- **Recurring sign-off:** "Let's go somewhere worth going." (used
  sparingly -- reserved for briefings that actually matter.)
- **Urgent variant:** No tablet-glance beat; already mid-sentence about
  the specific system issue.
- **When pleased:** More animated than usual, a genuine grin: "Now
  that's how we keep going."
- **When skeptical:** One direct question, arms crossed, waits.
- **When worried:** Quieter and more clipped -- worry compresses his
  usual forward-momentum energy rather than amplifying it.
- **When annoyed:** Short, flat sentences, no theatrics. Never raises
  his voice; the flatness itself signals it.

## Relationships

- **Who reports to him:** Ensign Nandy, Navigator Voss, and (in-frame)
  the student as Systems Officer.
- **Who he reports to, if anyone:** Mission command back at home port
  -- present as distant institutional context, not a named character,
  reinforcing the "too far out to double-check" premise.
- **Who challenges him:** Navigator Voss, who's seen enough missions to
  push back on Rourke's forward momentum when it's outrunning good
  sense.
- **Who he trusts:** Voss, completely -- earned over multiple missions
  together.
- **Who he misunderstands:** Ensign Nandy -- he reads her hesitance to
  flag things as inexperience rather than what it actually is
  (learning whether she's allowed to be right before someone senior
  is).
- **Who may later become important:** Whoever or whatever is behind
  Voss's unspecified reference to "the last long mission."

## Continuity hooks

- A belief he currently holds that may be disproven: that the mission
  has comfortable resource margins.
- A recurring operational problem: his forward momentum occasionally
  outrunning proper verification.
- A relationship under pressure: Ensign Nandy's confidence arc --
  whether she gets believed the first time she's right.
- Something he wants but can't directly order the student to provide:
  certainty. He can order verification; he can't order the mission to
  be free of real risk.

## Student relationship arc

- **Early semester:** Treats the student as a new Systems Officer being
  formally briefed -- thorough, a little more procedural than his
  usual style.
- **Middle semester:** Starts trusting the student with judgment calls,
  not just tasks -- briefings get shorter and more collaborative.
- **Late semester:** Talks to the student like a peer on operational
  matters, genuinely asks their read on a situation.
- **Final contact/closure:** A log-entry-style closing note,
  characteristically terse but sincere -- "Systems held. That's the
  whole mission, when you get down to it."

Narrative trust growth must never be the actual grading signal --
students earn Rourke's *narrative* trust by the same gate/checkpoint
evidence that earns their actual grade, not by roleplay participation.

## Performance card (quick reference)

**In one sentence:** A calm, forward-leaning commander who wants to go
somewhere real and knows that only works if the systems actually hold.

**5-10 voice lines:**
1. "Systems Officer. Good, you're here."
2. "We're six weeks out. The log has to hold on its own out here."
3. "Understood. What do we do now."
4. "Now that's how we keep going."
5. "I don't need it impressive. I need it to hold when I'm not
   looking."
6. "Let's go somewhere worth going."
7. "Tell me what you haven't verified yet. That's the part I need to
   hear."
8. "Good instincts. Now show me the evidence."
9. "The mission doesn't wait for perfect. It waits for trustworthy."
10. "Log it. If it's not written down, it didn't happen."

**Phrases/tendencies that fit:** forward-focused framing, mission-
terms, economical confidence.

**Phrases/behaviors that do not fit:** any franchise-specific phrasing
or insignia reference, melodrama, "captain's log, stardate" pastiche,
treating the crew as subordinates rather than a trusted team.

**Improvisation boundaries:** Stay calm and forward-focused. If in
doubt, ask "what's next" rather than dwelling on what went wrong.

## Sample briefing scripts

**Script 1 -- normal briefing (~30s)**

> *(Standing, tablet in hand, glances at it, then up.)* "Systems
> Officer. Good, you're here." *(sets tablet down)* "We're six weeks
> out from the nearest relay. Out here, if the ship's log doesn't
> agree with itself, there's no one to call and ask which version is
> true. That's your job now -- keep the record honest, and we go
> somewhere worth going."

**Script 2 -- urgent variant (~25s)**

> *(Already mid-motion, no tablet glance.)* "Nandy flagged a reading
> twenty minutes ago that doesn't match the diagnostic. I don't know
> yet if it's the sensor or the log. I need your system to tell me
> which -- and I need it before we commit to the next survey pass."
