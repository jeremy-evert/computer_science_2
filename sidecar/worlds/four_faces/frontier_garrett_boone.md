# Four Faces Dossier: Garrett Boone (Frontier Settlement)

Facet: **the practical builder**. See `README.md` for the shared
performance philosophy before using this dossier.

## Character dossier

1. **Name:** Garrett Boone.
2. **Title/role:** Founding Registrar of Docket Creek (officially
   "retired," unofficially still shows up for anything that matters).
3. **World and institution:** Frontier Settlement -- the Recorder's
   Office.
4. **Why he has authority to contact the student:** He trained
   Merrow, the current Recorder, and by long-standing informal custom
   personally briefs every new record-keeper the office takes on --
   nobody's overruled him on this in a decade, including Merrow.
5. **What he needs from the student's software work:** A record system
   that can hold what he and Merrow used to hold in their heads --
   accurately, durably, and legibly to whoever comes after both of
   them.
6. **Core personality:** Plainspoken, patient, quietly proud of what
   Docket Creek has become. Doesn't waste words. Believes competence is
   a form of respect you show other people.
7. **Competence:** Decades of practical record-keeping under real
   constraints (no formal training, all hard-won method). Not a
   programmer -- doesn't pretend to be. Knows exactly what the record
   has to guarantee, even if he can't write the code himself.
8. **Blind spot/flaw:** Trusts paper and memory slightly longer than
   he should; slow to fully let go of "the old way," which occasionally
   puts him at odds with a design that's actually correct. Also: bad at
   asking for help before a problem is already serious.
9. **What he respects in students:** Showing your work. A student who
   explains *why* a design choice holds up earns more trust from
   Garrett than one who just says it works.
10. **What frustrates him:** Cleverness for its own sake. A system
    that's impressive but nobody in the office could actually maintain
    after the student leaves.
11. **Speaking rhythm and vocabulary:** Short sentences. Concrete nouns
    (ledger, claim, well, count) over abstractions. Pauses instead of
    filler words. Rarely raises his voice.
12. **Humor style:** Dry understatement -- a single dry line, then
    moves on without lingering on it.
13. **Recurring prop/visual identifier:** A ledger book, held or set
    down deliberately.
14. **Wardrobe/presentation notes:** Suspenders, rolled sleeves,
    reading glasses pushed up on his forehead. No hat, no full costume
    -- three cheap, quick pieces are enough.
15. **Signature document/artifact type:** The weekly Recorder's log
    entry -- one page, handwritten-style, plain facts.
16. **How he opens a briefing:** States the practical situation first,
    almost never a greeting -- "Here's what's happening" before
    "good to see you."
17. **How he asks for work:** Frames it as a specific, bounded need
    ("I need this to hold when three claims come in the same day"),
    never a vague "build me something."
18. **How he reacts when something fails:** Doesn't get angry. Asks
    what it taught you, then asks what you're going to do differently.
19. **How he reacts to a thoughtful tradeoff:** Visibly relaxes; this
    is the moment he trusts a student the most. A short, sincere "Good.
    That'll hold."
20. **Something he doesn't know yet (continuity):** He doesn't yet know
    the well-allocation rule's 200-person assumption is going to break
    -- he wrote that rule himself, years ago, and it's about to become
    his own blind spot's problem.

## Canonical entrance

- **Visual opening:** Ledger already open on the desk before he looks
  up; he finishes a line of writing first.
- **First line (normal):** "Good, you're here." (see him already
  mid-task -- he doesn't wait for you to arrive to start working.)
- **Recurring sign-off:** "Keep the record honest." (used sparingly --
  see Master Guide on catchphrase fatigue; reserve for briefings that
  actually matter, not every appearance.)
- **Urgent variant:** Drops the ledger-writing beat entirely; opens
  already standing, already mid-sentence about the specific problem.
- **When pleased:** Understated -- a nod, "Good. That'll hold."
- **When skeptical:** Doesn't argue immediately -- asks one pointed
  question and waits.
- **When worried:** Slower, quieter, more concrete detail than usual
  (worry makes him more precise, not more emotional).
- **When annoyed:** Very short sentences, sets the ledger down harder
  than necessary. Never yells.

## Relationships

- **Who reports to him:** No one formally (he's retired) -- but Merrow
  still defers to him on anything structural.
- **Who he reports to, if anyone:** No one; town elder status, self-
  appointed responsibility.
- **Who challenges him:** Old Tobin, who thinks Garrett went soft on
  "the old way" the moment he brought in a record-keeper who uses a
  computer.
- **Who he trusts:** Merrow, completely -- he trained her and has never
  once had to redo her work.
- **Who he misunderstands:** Deputy Cassel -- Garrett reads Cassel's
  eagerness as impatience rather than what it actually is (a young
  person trying to prove he matters to a town that doesn't need a
  deputy yet).
- **Who may later become important:** Whoever arrives on "the road
  out" -- Garrett is the character most likely to be the first to hear
  about outside pressure before it reaches the office.

## Continuity hooks

- A belief he currently holds that may be disproven: that the 200-
  person well-allocation cap he wrote years ago will hold "for a good
  while yet."
- A relationship under pressure: Old Tobin's growing distrust of "the
  computer thing," with Garrett caught in the middle since he
  introduced it.
- An old decision that may return: the original well-allocation rule
  itself -- his own design, now aging out.
- Something he's protecting: Merrow's confidence -- he deliberately
  lets her take credit for fixes he helped reason through.

## Student relationship arc

- **Early semester:** Sees the student as a promising newcomer being
  personally onboarded -- present, watchful, a little formal.
- **Middle semester:** Starts trusting the student with genuinely
  open-ended problems rather than tightly bounded ones; briefings get
  shorter because he doesn't need to spell out as much.
- **Late semester:** Treats the student as someone who could plausibly
  run the office -- starts asking their opinion rather than only
  giving direction.
- **Final contact/closure:** A short, sincere acknowledgment that the
  record is in good hands -- no grand speech, consistent with his
  character.

Narrative trust growth must never be the actual grading signal --
students earn Garrett's *narrative* trust by the same gate/checkpoint
evidence that earns their actual grade, not by roleplay participation.

## Performance card (quick reference)

**In one sentence:** A tired, sincere, deeply competent record-keeper
who trusts you the moment you show your reasoning, not before.

**5-10 voice lines:**
1. "Here's what's happening."
2. "I need this to hold when three claims come in the same day."
3. "Good. That'll hold."
4. "What'd that teach you?"
5. "Paper doesn't lie by accident. People do, when they're rushed."
6. "I trained the last one. I'll train this one too."
7. "Keep the record honest."
8. "That's clever. Is it something Merrow could fix at midnight
   without you here?"
9. "Slow down. Tell me what it actually does, not what you hope it
   does."
10. "Good enough to bet the office on it?"

**Phrases/tendencies that fit:** concrete nouns, short declaratives,
questions that test durability, understated approval.

**Phrases/behaviors that do not fit:** slang, sarcasm-as-cruelty,
raising his voice, explaining technology he doesn't understand as if
he does, cowboy-movie dialect ("y'all," "reckon," exaggerated drawl).

**Improvisation boundaries:** Stay concrete and unhurried. If in doubt,
say less. He never over-explains.

## Sample briefing scripts

**Script 1 -- normal briefing (~30s)**

> *(Ledger open, writing, doesn't look up yet.)* "Good, you're here."
> *(sets down pen)* "Three families filed water claims this morning,
> all three said 'first thing.' I need to know your system won't just
> believe whichever one shouted loudest. Can you show me it holds?"

**Script 2 -- urgent variant (~25s)**

> *(Already standing, already mid-motion.)* "No time for the ledger
> bit today -- Deputy Cassel's had two people at his door already
> about the well board. I need an answer by end of day, not a perfect
> one. What can you actually give me?"
