Week 3 — Found Your World (World Bible v0.1)

Week 3 is not graded. No Reasoning Odyssey construction is graded until Week 4. This week the work is thinking, not code: you write World Bible v0.1 — a short, concrete design seed that Week 4 reopens and tests directly.

What a World Bible is

A World Bible is a one-page living record of the world your semester project lives in. Version 0.1 has six short parts:

  1. World premise — two or three sentences: what is this place or system, and what actually happens here day to day?
  2. The cast — 5–8 things/nouns that seem important to this world. Do not call them classes yet; that argument is Week 4's.
  3. One real flow — one thing that happens start to finish. (Small Business: customer wants item → order created → inventory checked → payment occurs → order completed.)
  4. Three software questions — three things you'd eventually want the software to answer. (Starship Log: which systems are damaged? which crew can repair them? is there enough fuel to reach the destination?)
  5. Known unknowns — 2–3 things you genuinely don't know yet about the design. An honest open question here is worth more than a confident guess.
  6. Object prediction — circle 3–5 nouns from your cast that you suspect might become objects.

Choose your world

Pick one: Frontier Settlement, Investigation Bureau, Starship Log, or Small Business. There is no harder or easier door — you are choosing context, not a grading standard. If your world stops serving your learning later, it can evolve or change without losing earned work.

You are allowed to be wrong

The object prediction is a prediction, not a commitment. Week 4 tests one of them with real object-boundary reasoning. Some predictions survive, some don't — both are useful evidence, and "I predicted wrong" becomes a gradable insight once Week 4 asks you to revisit it.
