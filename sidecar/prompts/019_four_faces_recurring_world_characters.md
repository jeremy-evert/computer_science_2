# Prompt 019: The Four Faces — Build Jeremy's Recurring World Characters

**Status:** OPEN  
**Course:** COMSC-1053 Computer Science II, Fall 2026  
**Owner:** Cleo  
**Scope:** Recurring in-world personas for Jeremy across the four Reasoning Odyssey worlds

## Mission

Build **The Four Faces**: four recurring characters Jeremy can actually perform, one for each living world.

Do not start from costume.

Start from character.

Each persona should feel like a real person with a job, authority, point of view, blind spots, habits, relationships, stakes, and a reason to keep contacting students across the semester.

The four characters should be distinct, but they should not require Jeremy to become four unrelated actors from scratch.

Use a stronger idea:

> Each character is an amplified facet of Jeremy.

Not literally Jeremy Evert transported through dimensions. Not parody versions of him. Instead, take four recognizable modes he already inhabits and turn one dial up in each world.

That should make the performances easier, warmer, more believable, and more sustainable across an entire semester.

## Dependency and source of truth

Read and respect:

- Prompt 015: CS2 slide system
- Prompt 016: Welcome to the Reasoning Odyssey
- Prompt 017: Four Living Worlds Bible
- Prompt 018: The Four Calls
- the current canonical world names, characters, institutions, tone, continuity rules, and student-facing boundaries in the repository

Prompt 019 should deepen the Jeremy-facing recurring characters without breaking accepted world canon.

If Prompt 017 already created strong candidate Jeremy personas, refine and reconcile them rather than replacing them casually.

## The four facets

Use these as creative anchors.

### Frontier world: the practical builder

Amplify Jeremy's **make-the-damn-thing-work** side.

This character understands that elegant theory is lovely until winter arrives, supplies run short, a bridge fails, a ledger lies, or three families all need the same resource.

He values:

- practical competence
- durable systems
- clear responsibilities
- making do without making excuses
- learning from failure
- decisions that survive contact with reality

Potential visual sparks include suspenders, work shirt, ledger, field notes, old-settlement materials, or a pipe used as a visual prop if appropriate and safe.

Do not make him a cartoon pioneer.

Give him dignity, humor, intelligence, and actual responsibility.

### Investigation world: the verifier

Amplify Jeremy's **"In God we trust; everything else we verify"** side.

This character cares about evidence, provenance, contradictions, assumptions, and whether the story survives scrutiny.

He values:

- evidence over confidence
- traceability
- careful observation
- reproducibility
- asking the next question
- separating what is known from what is merely claimed

This person should be useful for mysteries, disputed records, data problems, search, classification, inconsistent systems, and later callbacks.

Avoid hard-boiled detective parody unless used very lightly.

The authority should feel earned, not theatrical.

### Starship world: the commander/explorer

Amplify Jeremy's **we are going somewhere** side.

This character is ambitious, curious, forward-looking, and willing to attempt difficult things, but understands that bold missions still depend on systems that actually work.

He values:

- exploration
- capability
- technical courage
- disciplined experimentation
- teamwork between systems
- continuing the mission when assumptions fail

The existing Buzz-style name badge idea can be a delightful visual wink, but the character must not become a Buzz Lightyear imitation or copyrighted-character performance.

Build an original command persona Jeremy can own.

### Small Business world: the operator

Amplify Jeremy's **professor/chair/operator** side who understands that beautiful ideas eventually collide with customers, employees, money, deadlines, bad data, limited resources, and human beings.

He values:

- usefulness
- accountability
- sustainable operations
- understanding tradeoffs
- communicating with people who do not speak programmer
- making the system serve the organization rather than the reverse

This character should be able to surface very real software problems without turning the world into a generic MBA case.

Give him a business worth caring about and problems worth solving.

## Build actual characters

For each of the four personas, define at minimum:

1. **Name**
2. **Title / role**
3. **World and institution**
4. **Why this person has authority to contact the student**
5. **What this person needs from the student's software work**
6. **Core personality**
7. **Competence**
8. **Blind spot or flaw**
9. **What they respect in students**
10. **What frustrates them**
11. **Speaking rhythm and vocabulary**
12. **Humor style**
13. **Recurring prop or visual identifier**
14. **Wardrobe / presentation notes**
15. **Signature document or artifact type**
16. **How they open a briefing**
17. **How they ask for work**
18. **How they react when something fails**
19. **How they react when a student makes a thoughtful tradeoff**
20. **Something they do not know yet** that can support later continuity

## Performance must be sustainable

Jeremy should be able to perform these people repeatedly without needing elaborate production.

Design for:

- 30–90 second briefings
- one-take video when needed
- live classroom appearances
- a desk, office, hallway, or simple background
- one or two recognizable props
- wardrobe pieces Jeremy can put on quickly
- voices that come from posture, word choice, cadence, and attitude rather than difficult accents

Do not require impersonations, accents tied to ethnicity or nationality, or anything that could slide into caricature.

The ideal result is that Jeremy can put on one visual cue, shift posture, say one line, and students immediately know **who is calling**.

## Give each persona an entrance

Write a short canonical entrance pattern for each character.

This may include:

- a visual opening
- first line
- recurring greeting or sign-off
- how urgent messages differ from normal messages
- how the character appears when pleased, skeptical, worried, or annoyed

Avoid catchphrases that become exhausting after three uses.

Build recognizable behavior, not merchandise slogans.

## Give each persona relationships

The characters should not exist in a vacuum.

Place each one inside the cast from Prompt 017.

For each persona, identify:

- who reports to them
- who they report to, if anyone
- who challenges them
- who they trust
- who they misunderstand
- who may later become important

This gives future scripts somewhere to go besides "Jeremy character asks student to code another thing."

## Build continuity hooks

For each persona, plant several possible semester-long threads.

Examples:

- a belief the character currently holds that may later be disproven
- a recurring operational problem
- a relationship under pressure
- a technical system they trust too much
- an old decision that may return
- a mystery they cannot yet explain
- something they are protecting
- something they want but cannot directly order the student to provide

Do not lock the semester onto rails.

Give future authors handles.

## The student relationship must evolve

At the start of the semester, the persona may see the student as a new recruit, junior engineer, analyst, operator, consultant, specialist, or other world-appropriate role.

By later weeks, that relationship should be capable of changing.

Students should gradually feel more trusted because their demonstrated capability has grown.

Sketch an arc for each persona across:

- early semester
- middle semester
- late semester
- final contact / closure

Do not make narrative promotion dependent on roleplay participation. The academic work itself should justify the growing trust.

## Protect the academic firewall

These personas create flavor, memory, and motivation.

They do not control grading.

They must never:

- hide required technical instructions inside performance
- require students to understand private lore to complete graded work
- shame students who do not engage with the story
- create different academic standards across worlds
- contradict the shared Odyssey deliverables
- become substitutes for clear assignment instructions

When a character introduces a graded problem, the technical requirements must also exist in clear normal course language.

## Write for Jeremy, not at Jeremy

Do not make these characters stiff, over-scripted avatars.

Leave room for Jeremy to improvise.

For each persona, provide:

- a concise character card he can glance at before recording
- 5–10 lines that demonstrate the voice
- phrases / tendencies that fit
- phrases / behaviors that do not fit
- improvisation boundaries

The goal is not memorization.

The goal is to let Jeremy step into the character quickly and stay canonically recognizable.

## Deliverables

Produce:

1. a **Four Faces master guide** explaining the shared performance philosophy
2. one complete character dossier for each world
3. a one-page quick-reference performance card for each persona
4. wardrobe / prop guidance using inexpensive, practical items
5. signature briefing/document patterns for each persona
6. example opening and closing lines
7. several sample 30–90 second briefing scripts per persona
8. relationship maps tying each persona into the existing world cast
9. continuity hooks and unresolved threads
10. an early/mid/late-semester relationship arc for each persona
11. a reconciliation note showing how these personas align with Prompt 017 and Prompt 018
12. guidance for future agents writing dialogue in each voice

## Acceptance criteria

Prompt 019 is complete when:

- all four personas are distinct after removing costumes and visual props
- each character is grounded in a recognizable amplified facet of Jeremy
- Jeremy could plausibly perform each one repeatedly without elaborate production
- each persona has real authority, needs, relationships, flaws, and continuity potential
- each voice can be recognized from dialogue alone
- each persona fits the accepted world canon
- each persona can support The Four Calls and later narrative injections
- the student relationship can evolve as technical capability grows
- none of the characters becomes a grading gatekeeper or hidden source of required instructions
- future agents can write new scenes in-character without re-inventing the persona

## Explicitly out of scope

Do **not** in this prompt:

- build every world video for the semester
- write every future injection
- redesign the four world bibles
- alter the shared CS2 curriculum
- alter grading
- make normal classroom slides character-specific
- require roleplay from students
- build expensive costumes or sets
- imitate copyrighted characters

Give Jeremy four people worth becoming.

Then let them start calling.
