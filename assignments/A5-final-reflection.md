# Final reflection

## Purpose

Look back honestly on the semester — this is the final, in place of a traditional exam. Worth 10% of the course grade (`docs/grading-model.md`).

## Do

1. Pick **five** prompts from the bank below (or propose an instructor-approved equivalent) — at least one from each category.
2. Answer in your own words. Honest, specific answers pass; vague summaries do not, but there is no single “correct” answer.
3. Complete the Week 17 **Final Debrief** and Judgment Log/World Bible review as the Odyssey-specific portion. Do not write a second duplicate Odyssey reflection.
4. Share one favorite meme, quote, or moment from the semester — optional, but a nice way to close.

## Final Debrief — Coding Odyssey

Using concrete evidence from your World Bible, explain:

1. How did your world develop from its CS1 foundations (variables, control flow, functions, collections, and classes) through CS2’s exceptions, modules, files, inheritance, recursion, plotting, and searching/sorting?
2. Name one design decision that changed after testing, debugging, or comparing alternatives. What evidence changed your mind?
3. What broke, surprised you, or remained unfinished? Why?
4. If you restarted with what you now know, what would you preserve and what would you redesign?
5. Review the World Bible: is its charter, weekly gate record, known-debt list, and capstone choice sufficient for another person to understand the world’s evolution? Add any missing evidence.

## Prompt bank — pick 5, at least one per category

**Growth in programming**
- What can you now build or reason about that you could not at CS2 Week 1?
- Which CS2 concept took the most deliberate practice, and what finally made it click?
- How has your understanding of a class, an inheritance hierarchy, or recursion changed?

**A project or obstacle**
- Describe one exception, data, or design bug that taught you something real.
- Explain a visualization or search/sort result that changed how you saw your world data.
- Describe the hardest project decision and how you moved it forward.

**Learning habits and community**
- How did you help a classmate, or what support habit made you more effective?
- How did you use AI, documentation, tests, traces, or feedback responsibly when stuck?
- What would you tell a student entering CS2 after CS1?

**Next steps**
- What CS1/CS2 concept will matter most in your next course, project, or career step?
- Name one technical habit or portfolio improvement you will carry forward.
- What kind of problem do you now want to be able to solve next?

## Support and integrity

This is pass/fail and open-book/open-note/open-everything. Revise and resubmit if your thinking changes; specific evidence matters more than polished prose.
