# Reasoning Odyssey Gate — Week 3 — Found Your World (ungraded)

**Gate status:** optional_no_gate

Week 3 is not a technical gate and nothing here is graded. No Reasoning
Odyssey construction is graded until Week 4. What you do this week is write
**World Bible v0.1** — a short, concrete design seed that Week 4 will
directly reopen and test. This is the one week where thinking, not code, is
the actual work.

## Choose your world

Pick one: **Frontier Settlement**, **Investigation Bureau**, **Starship
Log**, or **Small Business**. There is no harder or easier door here — you
are choosing context, not a different grading standard, and you are not
required to continue any prior project. If your world stops serving your
learning later in the semester, it can evolve or change — more on that
below.

## Build World Bible v0.1

Keep this short. This is a seed, not a specification. Write:

1. **World premise.** Two or three sentences: what is this place/system,
   and what actually happens here day to day?
2. **The cast.** Choose 5–8 things/nouns that seem important to this world.
   Do not call them classes yet — that argument belongs to Week 4.
3. **One real flow.** Describe one thing that happens from beginning to
   end in this world. For example (Small Business): *customer wants item →
   order created → inventory checked → payment occurs → order completed.*
4. **Three software questions.** Three questions you'd eventually want this
   software to be able to answer. For example (Starship Log): *Which
   systems are damaged? Which crew members can repair them? Does the ship
   have enough fuel to reach the destination?*
5. **Known unknowns.** 2–3 things you genuinely don't know yet about how
   this should be designed. For example: *"I don't know whether Evidence
   belongs to a Case, or whether a Case just references Evidence."* An
   honest unresolved question here is worth more than a confident guess.
6. **Object prediction.** Look at your cast of nouns and circle 3–5 that
   you suspect might eventually become objects.

## You are allowed to be wrong

This is a prediction, not a commitment. Week 4 will test one of these
predictions directly, using real object-boundary reasoning — cohesion,
responsibility, state, and behavior. Some predictions will survive. Some
won't. Both outcomes are useful evidence, and "I predicted wrong" is a
legitimate, gradable insight once Week 4 asks you to revisit it.

## The door stays unlocked

If it turns out partway through the semester that your world isn't serving
what you're trying to learn, you can evolve it or change it without losing
work you've already earned. Don't over-think the choice above because of
this — it's a safety net, not a decision you need to plan around now.

Keep one concise World Bible entry: what you chose, why, and anything still
open. There is nothing to submit this week.
