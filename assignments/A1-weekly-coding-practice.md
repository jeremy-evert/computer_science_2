# Weekly coding practice

## Purpose

Reinforce this week's technical concept with real, working code, and show your process, not just your result. This is the “weekly topic reinforcement” artifact named in `docs/course-ethos.md`, worth 25% of the course grade (`docs/grading-model.md`).

## This is fulfilled by the Coding Odyssey, not a separate problem set

Every technical week is a Coding Odyssey week. The textbook's concept and vocabulary remain part of reading, lecture, and conversation, but practice happens by using the concept inside a persistent Odyssey world.

- **Gate weeks (2–5, 7–8, 10–13):** this assignment *is* that week's Odyssey Gate plus open continuation. Do `assignments/odyssey_gates/week-NN.md`; nothing else is due under this name.
- **Checkpoint weeks (6, 9, 14, 16):** this assignment *is* that week's Coding Odyssey checkpoint. See `assignments/A2-coding-odyssey-project.md`.
- **Weeks 1, 15, 17:** no chapter-concept gate. Week 1 is universal, Week 15 is an integration/polish week, and Week 17 is finals; see the matching `planning/week-NN.md`.

This implements the confirmed CS2 map: exceptions (Weeks 2–3), modules (Week 4), files (Week 5), inheritance (Weeks 7–8), recursion (Weeks 10–11), plotting (Week 12), and searching/sorting/Big-O (Week 13). Sources: `docs/curriculum/course-sequence.md`; Deitel, *Intro to Python for Computer Science and Data Science* (the corresponding topic sequence).

## Do

1. If you want a concrete starting point before improvising your scenario, work the **suggested textbook problem** in the week's gate file first. It is optional scaffolding, not a second assignment.
2. Do the current week's Odyssey Gate or checkpoint.
3. Submit working code, representative output, and a short explanation of what it does and why — apply the week's concept to your world, not in the abstract.
4. Demonstrate it in discussion or show-and-tell when scheduled.
5. Log it in your World Bible (`assignments/A2-coding-odyssey-project.md`).

## Grading — two axes, graded and reported separately

**Axis 1 — the working result (15 of 25 points).** For gate weeks: the Quick Check (pass/fail floor) plus the Light Build continuation band (Minimal/Solid/Strong — `docs/curriculum/judgment_toolkit.md` §1–2), mapped onto the historical effort ladder. For checkpoint weeks: the four-axis Full Build rubric (§2) stands in for this axis directly.

**Axis 2 — process (10 of 25 points).** Four dimensions every week, each scored 0 (absent) / 1 (present) / 2 (present and thoughtful), plus 2 points for an organized, reusable package:

| Dimension | What earns points | Evidence to submit |
|---|---|---|
| Rhetoric | Interrogated, restated, or justified the question rather than silently answering it | 2–3 sentences at the top |
| Planning | Planned before code and notes where reality diverged | Plan artifact, however rough |
| Resource budgeting | Tracks service/model/tier, limits hit, and why it fit | Tool-and-limits ledger |
| Knowledge management | Prompts and chats are findable and reusable next week | Prompt/chat log |

Resource budgeting is graded on tracking and reasoning, **never** on money spent; free/local tools can earn full marks. See `docs/grading-model.md`.

## Support and integrity

Ask questions in class, office hours, or the shared repository. AI is a tutor and debugging aid, not a typist (`docs/course-ethos.md`). You must explain and verify every submitted line and credit outside help or AI assistance.
