# Curriculum history synthesis

## Scope and method

I reviewed the Canvas JSON snapshots for all eleven archived semesters: spring 2021 through spring 2026, including fall 2021–2025. I compared assignment, discussion, quiz, page, module, and announcement names/descriptions, and skimmed the available uploaded text/document materials, especially the spring 2021 homework/project files and the spring 2026 syllabus/tutorial/homework materials. The archive is read-only source material; this synthesis is intentionally organized around recurring types rather than semester copies.

## What recurred

The durable later-course backbone is a chapter sequence: functions; strings and lists/dictionaries; classes (often two passes); exceptions; modules; files; inheritance; recursion; and searching/sorting. Chapter 16 is especially consistent, while plotting (Chapter 15) is intermittent until spring 2026. Fall 2021’s programming homework and readings show the same broad progression, though with a mix of Java/Python terminology; spring 2021 is the clearest outlier, with Java-oriented chapters, module pages, discussion homework, and chapter exams.

The dominant assignment type is frequent chapter coding practice, usually assembled from zyBooks labs. Recent versions move toward three problems plus output evidence and a quick reflection, sometimes allowing a student-designed problem. Open-ended CYOAG/Coding Odyssey projects recur from 2022 onward and ask students to apply several course tools to a game, utility, simulation, or other choice. Project Parts 1–2 add written reporting, peer comments, and a video presentation in the early archive.

Career/professional tasks are a second stable strand from 2021/22 onward: Dream Job, transcript/progress/degree planning, resume, gap analysis, GitHub, LinkedIn, and LinkedIn Learning. End-of-semester reflection is repeated with unusually permissive pass/fail language: choose five questions, answer honestly, and optionally add a meme/quote. Welcome videos, workspace photos, attendance/roll call, and “show and tell” signal community-building alongside technical practice.

## What evolved

The course moves from a 2021 discussion/exam/project model toward a Python/zyBooks chapter rhythm. CYOAG becomes Coding Odyssey and is sometimes repeated three times. Fall 2024 and spring/fall 2025 add a substantial driver/navigator pair-programming layer that explicitly permits peer or ChatGPT collaboration, plus “Coding Quest” duplicates of chapter work. Spring 2026 adds structured A01–A06 career artifacts, weekly mini-reports, and short productivity/wellbeing pages (Pomodoro, mindfulness, goals, journaling, scarcity/surplus); these are meaningful experiments but not yet evidence of the long-run CS2 core.

## Teaching-philosophy signals

The archive is practice-heavy and supportive rather than exam-heavy in later years. Rubrics often give credit for a serious attempt, then progressive credit for working solutions, and bonus for improvement, explanation, creativity, clean code, or repository/video evidence. The project framing values choice and practical application. Reflections and personal/professional tasks treat students as people with goals, and the collaboration/AI assignments emphasize process, driver/navigator roles, and learning rather than merely obtaining code.

## Inconsistencies and questions for Jeremy

- The archive has little stable syllabus/rubric structure: names, numbering, points, and even language/runtime shift across terms.
- Several snapshots contain unpublished placeholders, duplicate assignments, duplicated discussions, or stale links to external SharePoint/Google/Dropbox documents. Fall 2022 includes a placeholder giving full credit because submissions were missing.
- The archive’s quiz evidence is concentrated in spring 2021; later terms have no Canvas `quizzes` despite extensive chapter assessments/discussions. Decide whether quizzes were intentionally replaced or simply exported differently.
- Historical files are sparse and uneven: only some semesters preserve uploads, and the most consequential templates are often external links. Reconstructing exact original rubrics from the JSON alone is not always possible.
- Spring 2026 appears to include content from a broader professional-development/life-planning curriculum in the same Canvas course. Jeremy should decide which of those artifacts belong in CS2’s enduring identity.
