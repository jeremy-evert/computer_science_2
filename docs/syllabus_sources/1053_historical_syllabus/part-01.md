### Course Description

This course is a continuation of Computer Science I. Object-oriented programming concepts such as class inheritance, encapsulation and polymorphism are covered. Topics covered include abstract classes; interfaces; GUI programming; event-driven programming; data abstraction through use of classic data structures list, stack and queue; and object-oriented thinking and design. Emphasis will be on program design, modularity, debugging, and documentation. Source code management is practiced in paired programming Projects. F, S

### The Rules (said with love… but also with *eyebrow intensity*)

Attendance will be taken **regularly** and **at the start of every class**. If attendance is going to be a problem, you **must communicate** with me so we can find solutions. Life happens. I get it. But disappearing into the academic fog? **No. Not today.**

Because here’s the thing: life can be difficult… and it gets *way* more difficult when you feel disconnected. We meet regularly, we interact, we laugh, we wrestle the math gremlins together. I want you checked in — not just “technically enrolled.”

### How I’m tracking it

* **In-person / Zoom:** attendance gets taken at the **beginning of class**.

* **Online students:** we also check **Canvas activity** (last login/engagement).

You are expected to:

* Be in class **every day** (in person or via Zoom participation).

* Log into Canvas **every week** to submit assignments, take exams, or check announcements.

### The Grade Spice (this soup has consequences)

Good attendance helps your grade. Poor attendance hurts your grade. This is not a mystery novel.

**Perfect-ish attendance bonus:**

* **Less than 3 absences** *and* **no skipped weeks in Canvas** = **one-letter grade increase** at the end of the semester.

*(You want an A? Show up like you want an A. Next!)*

**Attendance penalties:**

* **6 absences** *or* **2 weeks of course inactivity** = **one-letter grade reduction**.

* After that? **Another letter drop** for **every additional week missed** *or* **every additional 3 absences**.

**Hard ceilings (the “soup limits”):**

* **3 weeks inactivity** *or* **9 absences** → best possible grade: **C**

* **4 weeks inactivity** *or* **12 absences** → best possible grade: **D**

* **5 weeks inactivity** *or* **15 absences** → grounds for **withdrawal**

*(You cannot ladle soup from an empty pot. And you cannot earn points from a class you are not attending. Next!)*

### Why I’m strict about it (the human part)

A lot of students learn more from **each other** than from the textbook. That magic doesn’t happen if you’re not in the room (or at least in the Zoom). Presence fuels discussion. Discussion fuels learning. Learning fuels passing. Passing fuels graduation. Graduation fuels “I can afford groceries.” It’s a whole ecosystem.

### Cameras (yes, we’re talking about it)

Last semester, students had strong feedback: **cameras on = better engagement**. Several people said when cameras were off, attention drifted. When cameras were required on, they stayed locked in.

I used to be against a camera policy because “camera on” means different things for different people:

* For some: it just means “don’t doomscroll.”

* For others: it can mean prep time, travel to better internet, and feeling drained afterward.

So here’s the deal:

* **In general, I’d like cameras on** for everyone — in class and in TV land.

* **I will be flexible and respectful.** If you need an exception, talk to me and we’ll handle it case-by-case.

* Our classroom has a camera, but your webcam is usually better.

* If you can join Zoom and participate, you can be **anywhere**. The classroom is open to you, but you’re not required to be physically there.
